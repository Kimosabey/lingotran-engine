**Dossier-langue** **Grammaire 10.4**

**The perfect tense (*le passé composé*)**

The perfect tense is a past tense which is used to refer to things that **have happened** and **are now over**.

**Hier, nous avons visité la ville.** *Yesterday, we visited the town.*

**J'ai acheté des cartes postales.** *I bought some postcards.*

The perfect tense is called the **passé composé** because it is composed of two verbs:

the present tense of *avoir* (this is known as the auxiliary verb) — a past participle (this gives the meaning)

**Nous avons ==passé== we (have) spent** <!-- "avons" is boxed in a plain white/outlined label connecting to "the present tense of avoir" arrow above it; "passé" is highlighted in yellow connecting to "a past participle" arrow above it -->

Find some more examples of verbs in the perfect tense.

Remember that **two** verbs are used to make the perfect tense in French.

**Stratégies**

**Translating French to English – past tense**

There are three ways to explain that something has happened and is now over in English, e.g.

*He helped in the kitchen.* &nbsp;&nbsp; *He did help in the kitchen.* &nbsp;&nbsp; *He has helped in the kitchen.*

When translating into French, there is only one way to say this, using the perfect tense:

*(handwritten-style font:)* *Il a aidé à la cuisine.*

How many ways can you say this statement and ask this question in English?
